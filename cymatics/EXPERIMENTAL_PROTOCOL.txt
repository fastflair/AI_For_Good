DNA CYMATICS — EXPERIMENTAL PROTOCOL

PURPOSE
Test whether a computational DNA-derived target can be reproduced by a real
mechanical/acoustic resonator and quantify the similarity.

A. COMPUTATIONAL PREPARATION
1. Enter the DNA sequence or retrieve it from the Gene / Sequence Retrieval tab.
2. Record sequence class, source identifiers, assembly, accession and SHA-256 hash.
3. Select the DNA conformation and structure source.
4. For sequence-only work, use the internal parametric heavy-atom model.
5. For a measured structure, prefer PDB/mmCIF coordinates.
6. Generate the literal axial projection and the chosen target projection.
7. Export the analysis bundle.

B. RESONATOR CHARACTERIZATION
1. Record resonator shape, radius/width, thickness and material.
2. Record mounting and boundary condition.
3. Record actuator type and position.
4. Record sensor/camera geometry.
5. Sweep frequency over the intended range.
6. Detect stable resonances and save their frequencies.
7. Photograph representative mode shapes.

C. DNA-TARGET TEST
1. Select a candidate exact physical resonance from the computational model.
2. Drive the real resonator at the measured/calibrated frequency.
3. Use fixed amplitude where practical.
4. Capture the cymatics image.
5. Record frequency, amplitude, timestamp and environmental conditions.
6. Repeat at least three times.

D. CONTROLS
Run:
- target sequence,
- shuffled sequence with matched length and base composition,
- reverse complement when applicable,
- periodic synthetic control sequences,
- neighboring frequencies above and below the candidate.

E. IMAGE ANALYSIS
1. Crop the resonator to the same geometry used for the computational target.
2. Normalize intensity.
3. Register translation and, if justified by the experiment, small rotation.
4. Calculate RMSE, Pearson correlation, Dice, IoU and boundary-distance statistics.
5. Compare polar/angular harmonic spectra.
6. Save the raw image and processed image.

F. INTERPRETATION
A pattern match supports geometric similarity under the chosen resonator conditions.
It does not demonstrate that the frequency is an intrinsic property of DNA,
that the mapping is unique, or that the result has biological significance.
