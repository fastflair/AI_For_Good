DNA CYMATICS — MODEL LIMITATIONS

1. Sequence-to-coordinate model
   The built-in generator is a pure-Python parametric heavy-atom geometry model.
   It is deterministic and explicit, but it is not a force-field calculation,
   molecular-dynamics simulation, quantum calculation, or experimentally refined
   structure.

2. Heavy atoms only
   Hydrogen coordinates are omitted by design. They are more sensitive to
   protonation, tautomer and local environment than the heavy-atom scaffold.

3. Sequence dependence
   B-DNA receives sequence-dependent local twist/rise and limited transverse
   shift/slide modulation. A/C/Z conformations use idealized family parameters.

4. A/B/C/Z labels
   A-DNA, B-DNA and Z-DNA are useful structural families. C-DNA is included as
   an exploratory geometric preset, not as a claim of a unique sequence-derived
   C-DNA conformation. The internal Z model is idealized and should not be treated
   as equivalent to a solved Z-DNA structure.

5. Uploaded structures
   PDB/mmCIF input is preferred when an experimental or externally validated
   coordinate set is available. The application uses those coordinates directly
   apart from deterministic axis alignment for visualization.

6. Projection
   A side projection of a long DNA molecule is expected to be line-like. The
   top-down, one-turn and phase-folded views are separate representations.
   Phase folding is a mathematical registration transform, not a camera image.

7. Cymatics target
   Atomic density is not a plate displacement field. The optional edge/nodal
   transform creates a comparison geometry for resonator matching.

8. Frequency interpretation
   A computed frequency is a property of the selected resonator model. It is not
   an intrinsic DNA frequency.

9. Real plate behavior
   Physical Chladni patterns depend on geometry, thickness, stiffness/tension,
   mounting, boundary conditions, damping and actuator coupling. The ideal circular
   membrane model is therefore a hypothesis generator.

10. Validation
    Image similarity is useful for pattern comparison but does not establish
    causality, uniqueness or biological significance. Controlled experiments and
    repeated measurements are required.
