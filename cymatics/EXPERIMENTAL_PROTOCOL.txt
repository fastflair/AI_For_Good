DNA CYMATICS — EXPERIMENTAL PROTOCOL
====================================

Purpose
-------
Test whether a precomputed molecular target can be reproduced as a spatial
resonance pattern of a defined physical resonator.

A. Pre-register the target
--------------------------
1. Record sequence identifier and SHA-256 sequence hash.
2. Record sequence class: genomic DNA, mature mRNA/cDNA, or CDS.
3. Record structure source: PDB/mmCIF, 3DNA rebuild, 3DNA fiber, or fallback.
4. Record DNA form assumption.
5. Record 2D target type and all projection parameters.
6. Record resonator geometry and model parameters.
7. Save the generated analysis ZIP before the experiment.

B. Characterize the resonator independently
---------------------------------------------
1. Measure plate/membrane diameter and thickness.
2. Measure or document material.
3. Document mounting and boundary condition.
4. Document actuator type and location.
5. Perform a frequency sweep over the intended range.
6. Measure vibration response with an accelerometer, laser vibrometer, impedance
   measurement, microphone or equivalent sensor where available.
7. Record resonance peaks and associated images.
8. Preserve the raw sweep and raw images.

Modern Chladni experiments show that the driving position and practical boundary
conditions can alter nodal patterns, and that mode mixing may occur at higher
frequencies. Do not assume the ideal analytical frequency is the measured
resonance.

C. Test one candidate mode
--------------------------
1. Select a single top candidate mode.
2. Use the exact unquantized physical frequency.
3. Do not use the musical WAV for the physical test.
4. Start at low amplitude.
5. Increase excitation only as required for visible pattern formation.
6. Record the exact frequency, amplitude setting and duration.
7. Photograph the pattern with a fixed camera.

D. Verify image geometry
------------------------
1. Upload the image in the Experimental Cymatics Verification tab.
2. Register only translation/small framing differences.
3. Record RMSE, spatial correlation, Dice, IoU, boundary distance and angular
   harmonic correlation.
4. Do not redefine or re-select the target after viewing the experimental result.

E. Controls
-----------
Use, at minimum, controls that separate sequence-specific geometry from generic
length and GC-content effects:

- GC-matched random sequence.
- Randomly shuffled sequence.
- Reverse complement.
- Periodic sequence with matched length.
- Canonical/idealized DNA-form reference.

Run controls through exactly the same structural, projection and resonator model.

F. Stronger inverse experiment
------------------------------
After resonance characterization, create a measured modal basis:

frequency_i → measured spatial pattern_i

Then solve for the sparse combination of measured modes that best matches the
pre-registered DNA target.

For each selected mode, record:

frequency, amplitude, phase, actuator position, image ID, resonance confidence.

G. Replication
--------------
Repeat the experiment with:

- fresh sample material,
- the same resonator on different days,
- and ideally a second resonator with independently measured geometry.

H. Data retention
-----------------
Keep all of:

- sequence FASTA,
- sequence hash,
- structure PDB/mmCIF,
- analysis ZIP,
- target image,
- resonator dimensions,
- mount description,
- actuator description,
- sweep data,
- raw camera images,
- processed images,
- sensor measurements,
- exact drive waveform,
- comparison metrics,
- software version.
