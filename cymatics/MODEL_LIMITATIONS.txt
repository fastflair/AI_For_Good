DNA CYMATICS — MODEL LIMITATIONS
================================

This file lists limitations that must be retained with experimental results.

1. Sequence-only DNA reconstruction
-----------------------------------
The built-in sequence-dependent reconstruction uses representative dinucleotide
step parameters. It is a geometric model, not molecular dynamics and not an
experimental structure determination.

2. 3DNA models
--------------
3DNA provides idealized fibers and atomic rebuilding tools. These are models and
should not be interpreted as the unique physical conformation of an entire gene.
A/B/C/Z form selection is a structural assumption/reference, not evidence that a
particular in-vivo locus has one global conformation.

3. RNA versus DNA
-----------------
The canonical hoxb1a-201 input is mature mRNA/cDNA. Modeling it as a DNA duplex is
an explicit computational choice. RNA and DNA differ structurally and should not
be silently conflated.

4. Long-locus projection
------------------------
A literal projection down the molecular axis and a side projection of a long DNA
sequence are expected to differ dramatically. A side view of a gene-length duplex
is visually line-like because the axial extent is hundreds of nanometers while the
diameter is roughly two nanometers.

5. One-turn target
------------------
The single-turn view uses only atoms in one axial pitch window. It is useful for
cross-sectional pattern analysis but is not a projection of every atom in the
gene.

6. Phase-folded target
----------------------
Phase folding uses a mathematical coordinate transform to register successive
helical turns. It is not a literal camera image. It is useful for repeat-geometry
analysis but must be labeled as derived.

7. Atom-density weighting
-------------------------
Uniform occupancy and atomic-mass weighting are alternative visualization models.
Neither is automatically equivalent to a camera intensity, electron-density map,
or mechanical force distribution.

8. Cymatics frequency mapping
-----------------------------
There is no universal DNA-to-Chladni frequency map in this application. Frequencies
come from the selected resonator model. A frequency has physical meaning only in
combination with the resonator geometry, material, thickness/stress, mounting and
actuator coupling.

9. Circular membrane assumption
-------------------------------
The Bessel-function mode solver is for an idealized circular membrane. A real metal
plate is generally a flexural plate with bending stiffness and practical boundary
conditions. Experimental resonances can differ from simple analytical modes.

10. Mode mixtures
-----------------
A visual mixture of several different resonant modes is an analytical visualization,
not one static physical Chladni pattern. Different frequencies generally produce
different time-dependent responses. Degenerate or nearly degenerate modes can mix,
which is why a calibrated resonator is preferable.

11. Musical WAV
---------------
The musical WAV is a sonification. Musical pitch quantization, harmony and added
harmonics are not guaranteed to preserve a physical cymatics pattern.

12. Experimental verification
-----------------------------
Image correlation does not establish causation, uniqueness or an intrinsic DNA
frequency. Controls, independent resonance measurement, repeated trials and full
metadata are required.

13. Uploaded structures
-----------------------
PDB/mmCIF uploads may contain multiple chains, ligands, solvent or proteins. The
current parser preserves all coordinates present in the uploaded structure. For
strict DNA-only analyses, upload a DNA-only structure or pre-filter the structure.

14. Numerical resolution
------------------------
Projection blur, raster resolution and mode-fit resolution affect image scores.
These are analysis parameters and must be retained in the analysis JSON.
