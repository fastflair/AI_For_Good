MODEL LIMITATIONS
=================

1. The internal heavy-atom builder is a deterministic geometric model, not a force-field, quantum-mechanical, crystallographic, cryo-EM, or experimental structure.

2. Mature mRNA/cDNA is not equivalent to genomic DNA. Using a cDNA sequence in the double-stranded DNA builder is a deliberate modeling construct.

3. A literal projection of an entire gene-length DNA molecule is elongated. Compact artwork requires a derived cross-sectional representation such as a one-turn, helical-phase, or rolling-turn projection.

4. The rolling-turn projection is sequence-wide but is not a literal photograph of the entire gene physically folded into a single turn.

5. Atomic-number weighting is an electron-count proxy, not a calculated electron-density map.

6. Gaussian atomic footprints approximate occupancy/visual density. They are not molecular scattering form factors.

7. The circular membrane and clamped circular plate modes are ideal analytical resonators. Real plates differ because of mounting, finite actuator area, damping, imperfections, tension, material anisotropy, and boundary conditions.

8. The inverse fit is performed in a restricted mode basis. An arbitrary 2-D image generally cannot be reproduced exactly by a small number of resonant modes.

9. The default displacement-power inverse is a first-order linear/time-averaged observable model.

10. The Nodal / sand model is a visualization proxy. Real powder/sand motion is nonlinear and depends on particle size, friction, adhesion, air drag, plate acceleration, and other effects.

11. A multi-tone drive does not automatically create one static Chladni pattern. Distinct frequencies can generate multiple spatial modes and time-varying interference; the primary WAV is therefore an inverse-model excitation, not a guarantee of one static image.

12. The actuator coupling factor is a controllability proxy, not a measured mechanical transfer function.

13. Calibration CSV data currently shifts frequencies to measured resonances and may apply response weighting. It does not identify phase, Q, damping, or complete complex frequency response.

14. Experimental image registration can improve numerical agreement but does not establish that the DNA geometry caused the observed pattern.

15. Strong validation requires a calibrated resonator, controlled boundary conditions, recorded drive parameters, repeated measurements, and comparison against measured mode shapes.
